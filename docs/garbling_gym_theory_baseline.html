Benchmark Report · review

The garbling gym becomes an instrument: a theory baseline that turns observations into measured deviations.

A new core/theory/ package computes the Bayesian-persuasion, cheap-talk, weak-institution, and Crawford–Sobel benchmarks for any configured game, so every gym run now measures deviation from a computed optimum rather than reporting a raw outcome. 01

Subject

Define the capability, the system under test, the workload, and the decision boundary.

SubjectIn: the garbling gym's new theory-baseline solver (Proposal 08).

A side-effect-free core/theory/ package computing the full benchmark ladder for any (prior, payoffs).

WorkloadWorkload: nine theory modules across two envelope dimensions, plus four closed-form oracles.

The 3-state / 3-signal / 2-action game and its binary-state collapse.

ExcludedOut: the commitment dial, sender learners, and the verifiability mask.

Those belong to Proposals 09, 10, and 11 respectively; this baseline only supplies what they measure against.

02

Methodology

Exact envelope computation over the belief simplex, validated against known closed-form results.

1Compute

Lift the sender value to the belief simplex and take the convex hull.

The commitment value cav is the upper surface of the convex hull of {(μ_x, μ_y, v(μ))}; the cheap-talk value qcav is the superlevel-set convex hull sup{s : μ ∈ conv{μ' : v(μ') ≥ s}}. Implemented in envelopes.py (1-D) and simplex2d.py (3-state).

2Interpolate

Solve the LRS weak-institution program by gridding γ.

For each credibility χ, maximize over (β, γ, k) subject to Bayes plausibility and the credibility-feasibility constraint; weak_inst.py. Crawford–Sobel N(b) is closed-form in cs_partition.py.

3Validate

Reproduce four closed-form oracles to ≤ 1e-3.

KG prosecutor–judge (0.60), LR ≤ KG pointwise, CS N(b) collapse (7→5→3→2→1), and 2-D interior consistency with exact boundary collapse to the 1-D solver.

04

Interpretation

The two findings that determine how downstream proposals must be configured.

Interpretation · primary finding

The gap is zero whenever the receiver buys at the prior.

Impact: At the default prior (0.3/0.4/0.3), E[BUY|prior] = 3.5 > 0, so the sender already earns the maximum (+10) with no information. Commitment and cheap talk have nothing to add — the entire interval Proposals 09–11 live inside collapses to a point.

Evidence: cav = qcav = babbling = 10.00 at both BUYS-at-prior configurations tested.

Resolution: Explained and bounded; not a bug.

Supporting finding

A LOW-heavy prior exposes a diagnostic gap of 5–10.

Impact: When the receiver passes at the prior, babbling and cheap talk both yield 0, but full commitment lifts the sender to 4.9–9.8 — a wide, measurable interval for χ_eff (P09), convergence targets (P10), and ρ_perceived (P11).

Evidence: The diagnostic prior (0.6, 0.3, 0.1) gives qcav = 0.00, cav = 6.29, gap = 6.29.

Resolution: Recommendation adopted in §06.

05

Limitations

Bound what the benchmarks can support.

01

Empirical-channel estimation is noisy at 20–50 rounds.

The realized channel L̂ carries a Dirichlet credible interval; consumers must not claim a Blackwell ordering the CI does not support.

Material
02

The LRS curve is solved by gridding γ.

Resolution trades accuracy for speed; the χ-discontinuity is reproduced qualitatively but its exact location is grid-dependent. Refine n_gamma for publication figures.

Bounded
03

The receiver has two actions (BUY/PASS).

This bounds how expressive the value function — and hence the gap — can be. The remedy is the prior override in §06, not an action-space change.

Bounded
06

Recommendation

Adopt the baseline and the diagnostic configuration.

Recommendation

Run Proposals 09–11 at the diagnostic prior (0.6, 0.3, 0.1).

It yields qcav = 0, cav ≈ 6.29 — a gap of ≈ 6.29 for credibility and commitment effects to land in. The default prior (gap = 0) is unusable for benchmark-anchored claims.
